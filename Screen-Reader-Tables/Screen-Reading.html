A breed is a paperwork category

Every cat on this page belongs to one species, Felis catus, named by Carl Linnaeus in 1758. The breeds below are categories that people created and still maintain. A breed standard is a written description of coat, body shape and temperament, and an organisation decides which cats meet it.

Two registries and two answers

The CFA, founded in 1906, accepts 42 breeds in its Championship Class. The TICA, founded in 1979, recognises 73. The same cat can hold a title under one registry and count as an unregistered housecat under the other. The two organisations draw the line in different places.

Reading the comparison table

Two columns in the table use terms from the registry standards instead of normal/standard English words. Their meanings are listed here.

menu_book What the body type words mean
Cobby
Short and compact, with heavy bone and a broad chest. The Persian is the usual example.
Oriental
Long, fine-boned and tubular, with a wedge-shaped head. Siamese and Sphynx sit here.
Semi-foreign
Between cobby and oriental, moderate in every direction.
Large
Substantially bigger than the average cat rather than differently proportioned.

Quick facts

Sleep
Twelve to sixteen hours a day, averaging thirteen to fourteen. Some cats reach twenty.
Lifespan
About thirteen years for a pet cat, up from roughly seven in the early 1980s.
Species
Every breed in the table is Felis catus, whatever the paperwork says.
